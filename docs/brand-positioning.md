# MRnObrainer Brand Positioning

## Core promise

MRnObrainer is the local-first desktop app that notices repetitive
digital work and suggests automations before the user knows what to
ask for.

## Audience

Primary audience:

- non-technical individuals
- AI-curious operators, founders, and busy professionals
- people who feel daily admin friction but do not want to assemble an
  automation stack by hand

Secondary audience:

- open-source contributors
- developers extending connectors, workers, and runtime behavior

## Product story

Most automation products assume users already know what they want to
automate. MRnObrainer starts earlier. It quietly captures daily
context, helps users see repeated work, then turns those patterns into
reviewable automation drafts.

The story on public surfaces should be:

1. Your day stays visible.
2. Repeated work becomes legible.
3. Good automation ideas appear with evidence.
4. Execution stays local by default and can expand later.

## Positioning rules

- Lead with the consumer benefit, not the architecture.
- Say "local-first" and "on-device" early.
- Present OpenClaw as optional scale, not required setup.
- Avoid opening with internal words like `pipes`, `workers`, `Oracle`,
  or `MCP` unless the audience is explicitly technical.
- Keep Screenpipe references as honest lineage, not the primary brand.

## Tone

- calm
- precise
- optimistic without hype
- premium, but still plainspoken

Good phrasing:

- "Automation for the work you forgot could be automated."
- "Drafts automations from your real day, not from a blank prompt."
- "Review first. Enable later."

Avoid:

- "AI agent platform"
- "workflow orchestration fabric"
- "autonomous productivity layer"
- "capture pipeline" on the main marketing surface

## Visual direction

- warm light backgrounds over dark cyber aesthetics
- restrained green as a trust accent, not a neon tech accent
- soft cards, clean hierarchy, generous spacing
- diagrams that feel product-level, not infra-level

The GitHub surface should feel like a consumer desktop product that
happens to be open source, not an open-source backend that happens to
have a desktop shell.
